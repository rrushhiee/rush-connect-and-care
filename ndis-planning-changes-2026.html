NDIS Planning Changes in 2026: Why Goal-Linked Support Matters

Published 25 May 2026 · By Rupert Rush

The biggest NDIS update to watch right now is the move towards a new way of planning, with support needs assessments expected to begin from 1 April 2027. The NDIA has said support needs assessments will become part of this new planning process, with the goal of making plans fairer, clearer, and more focused on a participant's actual support needs.

For participants, families, support coordinators, plan managers, and referrers, this makes one thing more important: everyday support needs to stay connected to goals, routines, independence, and real life.

Start an enquiry Back to articles

The NDIA has explained that support needs assessments are being developed as part of a new planning approach. The assessment is intended to gather information from participants, families, and carers about disability support needs, so budgets can be built in a more consistent way.

In plain English: the NDIS is trying to move away from a confusing paper chase and towards a clearer conversation about what support a person actually needs. That does not mean every participant will feel the change immediately. The NDIA has said the rollout will be phased, beginning with new participants from 1 April 2027.

Still, this is the signal worth paying attention to. If planning is becoming more focused on support needs, then providers also need to be clear about how their support connects back to those needs.

Why it matters

Support should be easy to understand, not hard to explain

Goals need to show up in real life

A goal on paper only matters if it connects to routine, confidence, community access, independence, or safer day-to-day choices.

Progress needs simple language

Families and referrers should be able to understand what support is doing without decoding a wall of provider jargon.

Consistency becomes evidence

Regular support, clear boundaries, and steady communication help build a picture of what is working and what still needs support.

This is where smaller, one-to-one support can have a useful role. Rush Connect & Care is not trying to sound like a giant agency. The strength is the opposite: practical support, direct communication, and a clearer relationship between the participant, family, referrer, and worker.

RCC stays aligned with NDIA updates by paying attention to the direction of the Scheme, keeping support connected to participant goals, and using simple documentation where it helps. That might look like progress notes, support summaries, or direct updates to a family member, support coordinator, or plan manager where appropriate and agreed.

The character of the work still matters. Support should not feel like someone has arrived with a clipboard and no pulse. It should feel steady, respectful, useful, and human. The paperwork matters, but so does whether the participant actually feels comfortable enough to engage.

For referrers

What this means when choosing support

Ask how support links to goals

Good support should be able to explain the connection between the session, the participant's needs, and the plan goals being worked towards.

Look for clear boundaries

Ethical support is clear about what it can provide, what it cannot provide, and when another service is more appropriate.

Value practical communication

Families, plan managers, and support coordinators often need clear updates, not overcomplicated language or vague promises.

The NDIA planning changes are not something to panic about. They are something to prepare for calmly. Participants do not need support workers who pretend to know everything before the rules are fully settled. They need providers who stay current, communicate clearly, and keep the support grounded in real needs.

That is the lane Rush Connect & Care is building in: practical one-to-one NDIS support and youth mentoring that stays personal, goal-linked, and aware of the bigger system around the participant.

Official NDIA information is available through the NDIS pages on the new way of planning, support needs assessments, and participant testing of the planning process.
