Mental health and frustration

Managing Frustration When Mental Health Feels Heavy

Published 19 June 2026 · By Rupert Rush

Frustration can be one of the hardest parts of mental health. It can build quietly, sit in the body, sharpen thoughts, and make ordinary things feel heavier than they should.

When someone is hearing voices, feeling stuck, or trying to manage mental health problems, frustration can also become a signal. It may be the mind saying: this is too much right now. The goal is not always to force it away. Sometimes the first step is knowing it will pass with time.

What it can offer is a grounded way to think about frustration: the kind that comes when the mind feels loud, when progress feels slow, or when a person is tired of fighting themselves.

Rush Connect & Care is built around lived experience, practical support, and the belief that people can learn from what they have been through. Healing is not always neat. Sometimes it is the slow process of becoming less afraid of your own mind.

Letting it pass

Frustration can feel permanent, but it usually moves

Time changes the temperature

When frustration peaks, the mind can treat the moment like the whole future. Waiting, breathing, walking, or stepping away can let the heat come down.

Not every thought needs action

A frustrated thought may be loud, but loud does not always mean true. Sometimes the strongest response is to pause before acting.

The body needs a way out

Movement, cold water, quiet space, music, or getting outside can help the nervous system shift before the mind has all the words.

Close people

The people around you can help carry the moment

When mental health is difficult, isolation can make frustration echo. The mind loops. Small problems become symbols. A comment, a delay, or a feeling can suddenly seem bigger than it is.

This is why the people close to you matter. A parent, friend, sibling, carer, mentor, support worker, or trusted person can help interrupt the loop. They do not need to have perfect answers. Often their value is simpler: they stay nearby while the feeling passes.

Support does not always need to sound profound. Sometimes it is a drive, a walk, a normal conversation, or someone saying: let us get through the next hour first.

Hearing voices

Help for hearing voices starts with safety, support, and reducing fear

Try to understand the pattern

People often search how to fix hearing voices, but a safer first goal is learning what makes the voices louder, softer, easier, or harder to manage.

Use grounding and routine

Structure, sleep, movement, food, reduced isolation, and having a plan for difficult times can help some people manage hearing voices more safely.

Bring in the right support

If voices are distressing, threatening, or telling someone to harm themselves or others, professional and crisis support should be contacted immediately.

Creativity

The mind can use past experience to build something new

One strange thing about mental health recovery is that the past can become material. Not material in a neat way, or a romantic way, but in the sense that a person can learn from what their mind has survived.

Frustration can sometimes point to a blocked need: rest, control, expression, connection, movement, honesty, or safety. Once a person starts recognising the pattern, they can become more creative with how they respond.

As humans, we learn. We adapt. The subconscious mind stores fear, memories, warnings, and old pain, but it can also learn new routes. With time, support, and repeated safe experiences, the mind can begin to understand that not every old alarm means danger now.

Practical reset

Small ways to manage frustration before it takes over

Name the feeling

Say it plainly: I am frustrated. I am overwhelmed. I need a minute. Naming it can create a little distance from it.

Change the scene

Move rooms, go outside, sit in the car, walk around the block, or step away from the conversation before it becomes bigger.

Return when calmer

Many problems are easier to solve after the body has settled. Waiting is not weakness. Sometimes it is the most intelligent thing available.
